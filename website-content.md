## How comparable sites do it (and what we're borrowing)

| Site | What it is | What we borrow for K21 |
|---|---|---|
| **CFA Institute – CFA Program** | A notoriously hard, globally recognised exam with set exam windows | Treat the exam as a *prestige credential*: "gold standard" framing, a clear numbered path ("pass the exam → …"), a "Who should take it?" block, and an FAQ that handles the fine print. Difficulty is shown through commitment (levels, hours) rather than just called "hard". |
| **TCS iON National Qualifier Test (NQT)** | One test whose score is used by many companies to shortlist candidates | Link the exam to hiring with a **step-by-step journey** (register → test → score → companies → hired), a "who can apply" list, a stated exam frequency, and honest small print ("a score improves your chances, it doesn't guarantee a job"). |
| **Minerva University** | A selective, global university with "the city is your classroom" learning | **Contrast headlines** ("This isn't X. It's Y."), a global classroom told through stories rather than a list, skills named explicitly (critical thinking, communication…), and proof placed right after every bold claim. |
| **Cambridge International** | A global curriculum used in 160+ countries, with stages by age | **Stage-based pathway** (age/level → what you learn) and global reach shown through country pages and recognition rather than one number. |
| **42 Network** | Tuition-free coding schools with no diploma needed and a demanding selection process | **"Open to anyone, earned by few"**: low entry barrier plus a tough selection. Short punchy triads ("No X. No Y."), and a mission line about access "for all, everywhere". |

**Patterns we're using across the site:**
1. Every bold claim gets a one-line *how* or *why* underneath it, never a buzzword on its own.
2. A clear journey from Age 12 to career, shown as steps.
3. Exclusivity (1K seats) is paired with openness (7th standard minimum). That tension is the hook.
4. Repeated, consistent CTA: **Book Your Seat**.
5. FAQ handles eligibility, schedule, recognition and fine print.

## 3. THE K21 EXAM

### Hero
**Headline:** One of the toughest competitive exams in the world, by design. `[#2]`
**Subheadline:** One exam that measures knowledge across 12 engineering disciplines, business and finance against one global standard. `[#3]`

### Why make it this tough? `[#2] [#7]`
Easy exams produce certificates. Tough exams produce *proof*.

The K21 exam is deliberately demanding because its result has to mean something: to universities, to employers and to you. It is modelled on the **selection methods used by high-end global companies** to hire. That means testing reasoning under pressure, applying knowledge to unfamiliar problems, and showing how you think as well as what you remember.

### What the exam covers `[#3] [#6] [#11] [#14] [#12]`
- **Engineering breadth.** Knowledge drawn from twelve engineering disciplines, so that one exam reflects the range of what K21 learners study. (**⚠ CONFIRM** which 12.)
- **Business & administration.** How organisations, markets and governments work across 193 countries.
- **Financial management.** Budgeting, investment basics, financial decision-making and reading the numbers behind a business.
- **Reasoning & aptitude.** Creative, analytical and logical thinking: the IQ power K21 trains.
- **Applied problem-solving.** Real-world scenarios in the style of global company selection rounds.

### How often? Every six months. `[#13]`
The K21 exam is held **once every six months**. That gives you:
- Two chances a year to take the exam or improve your result
- Enough time between sittings to genuinely learn, not just cram
- A regular, predictable rhythm you can plan your studies around

(**⚠ CONFIRM** exam months, mode (online / centre-based) and whether retakes are allowed.)
